Patterns & Parameters

A pattern is one visual look. The library is organized into themed groups, with audio-reactive effects and a deliberately non-reactive checkerboard. Click one and it appears on screen instantly.

Browsing the library

The pattern library sits in the middle of the control panel. Patterns are grouped by theme:

  • Simple — first in the library: Circles, Bars, Dot Grid, Pulse Stripes, Cross Pulse, Diamond Tiles, Radial Spokes, Triangle Mesh, Ring Grid, Hatch Weave, Dash Lanes, and Checkerboard.
  • Rhythmic — beat-driven shapes like Pulse Rings, Beat Weave, Ripple Lattice, Pulse Grid, Wave Stack, Beat Orbit, and Level Blocks.
  • 3D — rotating, depth-mapped structures, including Polygon Tunnel, Orbital Cages, Helix Tower, Perspective Floor, Gyro Rings, and Depth Frames.
  • Cinematic / Shaders — GPU shaders and atmospheric looks, including lightweight Silk Flow, Prism Caustics, Ember Drift, Velvet Fog, Prism Flare, and Molten Glass.
  • Neon / Lasers — glow lines and light effects, including Laser Fan, Neon Hex, Laser Harp, Neon Frame, Beam Cascade, and Circuit Pulse.
  • Video FX — camera-input effects like chroma key, kaleidoscope, edge glow, thermal color, prism split, ripple lens, mirror tiles, halftone, solarize, wave warp, and duotone.
  • Glitch / Effects — RGB split, scanlines, CRT, Data Rain, Signal Tear, Pixel Sort, VHS Tracking, Block Shift, and Interference.
  • Basics — solid color, color wash, color bars, noise static, film grain, Vignette, Split Tone, Sweep Band, and Grid Lines.
  • Alphas — white/gray looks on a pure black background (Alpha Rings, Alpha Bars, Alpha Grid, Alpha Spot, Alpha Sweep, Alpha Diamonds, Alpha Fog, Alpha Waves), built for Alpha Blend in projection mapping: brightness becomes opacity once the black keys out.

Fast building blocks

The Simple shapes use bounded, single-density Canvas2D drawing. The shader and camera effects use single passes instead of heavy raymarching. Each has bass, mid, and high responsiveness controls; set a band to zero to remove its contribution. Hover a library button to see what each band changes.

Checkerboard is not audio reactive. It starts as stationary black-and-white squares. Change cell size, hues, saturation, lightness, or optional manual drift. Neither audio nor old saved audio-pulse settings can change its size or speed. It stays in Simple and is the default source when you add a mapping inside a Projection Mapping pattern; existing mappings keep their sources.

Camera effects require permission on the output window; the control preview is a placeholder. The new effects include a mirror switch and share one camera stream across LIVE and CUE. Thermal is a false-color visual effect, not a temperature sensor.

Searching the library

Press / to jump to the search box above the list, then type. Matching ignores case and covers each pattern's name, id, group, description, and type keywords — so camera finds the Video FX patterns, media the loaded images and videos, and mapping the projection patterns. Separate terms all have to match, so video tiles narrows the list instead of widening it.

A count line shows how many patterns matched, and a query that matches nothing offers Clear search. Groups with results expand automatically while you type; your saved collapsed groups are never changed. Esc in the field clears the query, then leaves the field.

Favourites

Click the ☆ at the right of any library item to favourite it; it turns ★ and the pattern is pinned in a Favourites group at the top of the library, in the order you added them. Click the star again to remove it. Favourites are per-browser UI state, like collapsed groups — they are not part of a pattern's saved parameters — and they travel with exported settings.

A favourited pattern also stays in its own group, so pad keys, drag-and-drop order, and Shift+click cueing behave the same in both places. While a search query is active the Favourites group is hidden, so a result never appears twice.

Tweaking parameters

Every pattern exposes its own sliders. The Parameters panel on the right updates to match the selected effect — bass/mid/high responsiveness, counts, speeds, and more. Drag a slider and the change applies to the output live, so you can shape a look while it's on the projector.

Tip Most patterns react to three frequency bands — bass, mid, and high. The responsiveness sliders control how strongly each band drives the visual.
